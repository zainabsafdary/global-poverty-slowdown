# global-poverty-slowdown
Analysis of why the fall in global extreme poverty slowed after 2015, across 195 countries and regions.

I used World Bank poverty data (1990–2025) at the $3.00-a-day poverty line to look at why progress stalled. The analysis:

- breaks down the change in the world poverty rate by region
- separates poverty changes in household surveys into the effect of income growth and the effect of changes in inequality
- tests whether growth reduces poverty less in Sub-Saharan Africa

East Asia drove most of the decline before 2015. By 2025, about seven in ten of the world's extreme poor lived in Sub-Saharan Africa, where average incomes stopped growing after 2015.

The analysis was completed in **Python**, using `pandas`, `numpy`, and `matplotlib`.

The full analysis and visualization code is available in: `poverty_stall_analysis.py` and `make_charts.py`

Data: World Bank Poverty and Inequality Platform, via [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/poverty). Save `complete_series.csv` in the same folder as the scripts.
